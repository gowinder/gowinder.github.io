# Nicolas Cage in The Family Man


![The Family Man](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/M/MV5BYzYzMTQ4NDYtNWRkZi00NTYxLThlZDItMzI1MmU1MmU5ZWQ3XkEyXkFqcGdeQXVyNDA5Mjg5MjA@._V1_FMjpg_UX1000_.jpg)

When I watched this film I was on a long business trip away from home. I wouldn't say my career was really on the up, but at least I hadn't yet thought about going back to Wuhan.

I saw it on an ordinary weekday, after work, with nowhere to go, alone in the office. It runs less than two hours, and yet it felt as though I had lived through a good long stretch of time alongside the protagonist, Jack. Over that stretch Jack goes through an illusory experience, transforming from a rich bachelor who has everything and therefore nothing into a homebody who puts family above all.

I have to say the plot of this film is utterly absurd and simply could not happen. And yet I believe that if Jack had made the choice, at the very start of the film, to stay with his girlfriend, then maybe his life really would have unfolded along that invented storyline.

A person's choices decide his fate. Once Jack gives up his girlfriend he rises steadily, until he has his own patch of territory in a high-rise Wall Street office, different women throw themselves at him every day, and the closet in his spacious bedroom holds countless designer outfits for him to pick from, as if he ran a clothing shop himself. There's a long take in the film where Jack, humming a Pavarotti tune while he showers, opens his ultra-luxurious closet, casually picks out some exquisite garment, and then stands in front of the mirror. My god. I remember thinking at that moment: maybe this is the life I want. No — this is the life every Chinese person wants.

If the film had been nothing but the life of a rich bachelor, I'd probably have forgotten the story the next day, and it would have lowered Cage in my estimation as well.

In fact Cage didn't let me down, and the good part comes later: on Christmas Eve, Cage is alone in a small supermarket (a rich bachelor doesn't have much family, after all) when he runs into a black man who is holding up the place at gunpoint because he wrongly believes Jack forged his lottery ticket. To avoid trouble, Jack settles the matter with his own money. In return the man gives him a bell and says a whole lot of things that leave Jack completely baffled. Jack goes home, puts the bell on the bed, and falls asleep.

At this point the story opens a new thread, and one I absolutely did not see coming: the next morning he is woken up by his own daughter and the dog. Strange — wasn't he a rich bachelor? The film now drops Jack into the life he would have led had he never left his girlfriend, a life of settling down and living happily ever after.

If a man's whole life turns upside down within a single day, he'd surely go mad. And if he goes from being a rich bachelor who lacks nothing to a family man who is harassed by the kids every day and frets about money every waking hour, what then?

Jack falls apart. Everything he had no longer belongs to him — his apartment, his sports car, his status, his women, all of it gone, replaced by children who make a racket every day, a wife who nags at him nonstop, and a boring job selling car tyres.

So Jack starts looking for a way out. He finds the black man, who has taken over Jack's position, and who tells him that when the bell rings again Jack will return to his former life.

I don't feel like telling the rest of the story — go and watch the film yourselves. The ending is a lot of fun anyway.

After finishing it I couldn't calm down for a long time. There was too much to think about: what kind of life do I actually want, what am I really chasing? A high-quality material life is what everyone wants, but if the price you pay for it is spiritual isolation and poverty, how do you choose?

Maybe most people are like Jack after he becomes an ordinary man — tedious, bored, helpless, forever fantasising about making it big, and nobody ever stops to ask what the cost of that kind of life might be.

Are we willing to give up our families, to give up the people who care about us, for money? In a society like China's, where money is everything, what actually matters most? What is really your own choice?
