# Wuhan's Traffic Has Become a Bottleneck for the City's Growth!


Yesterday a colleague gave me the bad news: Changqing Road is being closed — closed for 52 days — and the 800,000 people over there will all be funnelling through Gusaoshu Road, which I pass every single day.

God. It already took me nearly two hours to get from Hankou to Wuchang for work every day, and now this. Ugh.

Sure enough, I didn't get home until after eight last night. What a jam. I really don't know how a developing country like China has so many cars!

![](http://img1.qq.com/hb/pics/12117/12117161.jpg)

Today I read the news online. The reporter said: 3 kilometres took 40 minutes. Original article: http://hb.qq.com/a/20080806/000042.htm

And then a traffic cop actually says **"the traffic volume on these roads was already close to saturation on a normal day; yesterday's congestion was still within an acceptable range."**

Ugh. Speechless. Chinese people are numb to this too!

**"According to reports, to support these traffic control measures the traffic management department deployed more than 200 police officers to direct traffic, and set up over 100 diversion posts and 220 direction signs along the route, effectively relieving pressure in the controlled area."**

So the police were there — I just didn't see 200 of them. I'd be surprised if there were 20!

**"The reporter learned on site that quite a few drivers still didn't know about the closed construction here. Some saw that buses and taxis could get through, so they 'played dumb' and drove in. One traffic officer explained that to avoid blocking the intersection by stopping cars there, they did not force a stop while the side lanes were still flowing fairly normally — but that doesn't mean there are no electronic police cameras inside the restricted zone. Vehicles that barge in will face penalties."**

Let's not even talk about their ability to solve traffic problems. Fining people and collecting money — first class, that.

**"The Wuhan Traffic Management Bureau and the contractor, China Railway 11th Bureau Group, explained that the full-closure plan was decided after weighing the options repeatedly: the construction side drew up three plans, requiring half-closure, full-closure and half-closure respectively, with construction periods of 104 days, 52 days and 192 days. With a half-closure, the already heavy traffic would all be squeezed onto half the road surface, and conditions would inevitably get worse; on top of that the construction period would be longer, so it was not the better choice. The full-closure plan needs the shortest construction period. Bearing in mind that 'a long pain is worse than a short one', they decided on the second plan."**

Poor drivers. We pay every tax under the sun, the bridge is only crossable 365/2 days a year, and now the roads aren't usable either — all we can do is squeeze onto the rest.

This morning I left 20 minutes early for work, thinking I'd arrive early. Jams everywhere: Shiqiao, Tangjiadun, Beihu, Hangkong Road, the Yangtze River Bridge, Yuemachang, Fujiapo... The subway — when on earth will it be finished?
