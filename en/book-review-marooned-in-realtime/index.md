# Book Review: Marooned in Realtime


![Marooned in Realtime](https://img2.doubanio.com/view/subject/l/public/s29930241.jpg)

[http://book.douban.com/review/3801851/](http://book.douban.com/review/3801851/ "http://book.douban.com/review/3801851/")

"Marooned in Realtime" is obviously the sequel to "The Peace War".
The style is different — Realtime reads a lot like a detective novel — but I don't think the way it's told is all that different.
Vinge's novels always spend a huge amount of the early going laying out the setting, the characters, the clues and so on, so with a Vinge novel you have to get at least halfway through before it becomes impossible to put down. "Realtime" and "The Peace War" are both like that, and so was "A Fire Upon the Deep", which I read before: the beginning was so dull, I spent days on the first half, reading a bit and stopping, and then I finished the second half in one sitting. The real climax is at the back. It makes you not want to put it down.

Right to the end the story never explains the cause of the "Great Extinction" — whether humanity partied itself to death, or it was aliens, or, as Della Lu thinks, it was a transcendence. A lot of novels carry that idea, for instance Liu Cixin's "Supernova Era", where once humanity is elevated the physical body just disappears. I wonder whether Vinge will write another one about the cause of the "Great Extinction", and more likely it wouldn't be about the original extinction but about a second one, suffered by the new society that Della Lu and the others built afterwards, using that to infer the cause of the first. If he really does write it, I'm sure Vinge will surprise me — his novels have basically never let me down. In the end I was going to go buy "A Deepness in the Sky" and "A Fire Upon the Deep", but there were no new copies left, so I'll see if I can hunt down second-hand ones.

Next month's big releases are "Three Body" 3, and of course COD7, ha. For now I'd better hurry up and read other things. And pick up a few more sci-fi books. I didn't buy that Vinge short story collection last time — I'll make up for it this time.
